Demand / Slutsky forced

A linear price tilt of the forced law makes mean choice the envelope of $\log Z$. Slutsky symmetry and negativity are that scalar’s convexity. The dividend is measured asymmetry: when $\mu$ carries price, the antisymmetric part estimates the reference.

The paper recovers the demand system as the envelope derivative of the log-partition function under $V(x)=-p\cdot x$ (Table 3; section 5; film I). No utility is posited. Income effects live in a richer $V$ that encodes the budget, and are not claimed here.

$$x^*(p)=-\tau\nabla_p\log Z,\qquad \frac{\partial x^*}{\partial p}=-\tau\nabla_p^2\log Z$$

When $\mu$ is price-independent the Hessian is $-\mathrm{Cov}(x)/\tau$: symmetric because mixed partials of a scalar commute, negative semidefinite because $\log Z$ is a convex cumulant generating function. When the reference carries price, $\mu=\mu(x;p)$, the response is

$\frac{\partial x_i^*}{\partial p_j}=-\frac{1}{\tau}\mathrm{Cov}(x_i,x_j)+\mathrm{Cov}\bigl(x_i,\partial_{p_j}\log\mu\bigr)$

The first term is symmetric, so all measured asymmetry sits in the second. That is the estimator.

What the paper states

From section 5: with a linear value tilt $V(x)=-p\cdot x$, the mean choice is $x^*(p)=-\tau\nabla_p\log Z$, the demand system; its response is the Slutsky matrix $\partial x^*/\partial p=-\tau\nabla_p^2\log Z$. Both textbook properties follow in one stroke: the matrix is symmetric because mixed partials of a scalar commute, and negative semidefinite because $\log Z$ is convex in $p$ as a cumulant generating function. Slutsky symmetry and negativity are the single fact that $\log Z$ is a convex cumulant generating function, with no utility posited.

When $\mu=\mu(x;p)$, the first covariance term stays symmetric, so all asymmetry in a measured demand Jacobian sits in $\mathrm{Cov}(x_i,\partial_{p_j}\log\mu)$. Under the maintained linear tilt and constant $\tau$, the antisymmetric part is an estimate of the reference’s price dependence.
