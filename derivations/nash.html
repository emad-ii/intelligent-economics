What the paper states

From section 5.2: when several agents choose against one another, $V_i=V_i(x_i,x_{-i})$. The object has each agent relax to a distribution against the others' distributions, $\rho_i\propto\mu_i\exp(\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{-i}}[V_i]/\tau)$. A joint fixed point of these coupled relaxations is a quantal response equilibrium (McKelvey and Palfrey 1995), which, in finite games, exists for every $\tau>0$ by Brouwer's theorem, the joint best-response being a continuous self-map of a compact convex set. The existence argument is cleaner than the one Nash equilibrium itself requires, because the softmax response is single-valued where the sharp best-response correspondence needs Kakutani.

Nash equilibrium is the $\tau\to 0$ limit, where the relaxation sharpens to exact best response and the quantal fixed point limits onto a Nash profile of the $\mu$-supported game, best response among the configurations the references carry. Strategic interaction is therefore the object with coupled value functions, and its solution concept is the bounded-rational equilibrium with Nash as the zero-temperature corner. Table 3 lists the recovery as forced, recovered by the $\tau\to 0$ limit of coupled tilts (QRE), and the interest it returns as equilibrium selection by the reference. Film III on ie.ii.inc is the zero-temperature limit.

The paper locates the prisoner's-dilemma cooperation puzzle on the same object: defection is the equilibrium on a disconnected, no-future interaction, and cooperation is what a connected, repeated interaction against a shared reference supports. That reading is not simulated here.
