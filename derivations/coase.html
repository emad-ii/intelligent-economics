The market and the firm identified

A firm is a maintained $\mu$ joined to a joint value function and a residual claimant. The market is coordination at minimal task-specific shared doxa; the firm is coordination by deep shared doxa. The two are poles of one trade-off: maintain a reference, or pay per-transaction tilts across the gap.

The paper begins from Coase's question — why islands of conscious coordination persist inside the price system [Coase, 1937] — and treats his accounting answer as accounting. The primitive is the firm as a deliberately built and continuously maintained $\mu_{\mathrm{firm}}$ (section 6.2; the Coase slider on ie.ii.inc). This is not a Table 3 row. No Kramers rate is written in the paper, and none is added here.

What the identification transfers

Deepen the shared doxa (lower $\Delta$) and the market pole cheapens: price can carry the margin against a thinner gap. Raise the gap, or the load, and paying to maintain a reference wins. That is Coase 1937 as a depth, not as a rate. Coase 1960 sits one section earlier: when reallocation is cheap the relaxation reaches $\rho^*$ from any start; when it is dear, initial allocations matter. That reading is not a second interactive.

What the paper states

From section 6.2: the theory of the firm begins from Coase's question (why do islands of conscious coordination persist inside the price system [Coase, 1937]) and his answer, that firms exist where using the market costs more than using authority, holds as accounting; the primitive lies deeper. A firm is a maintained reference joined to a joint value function and a residual claimant: a deliberately built and continuously maintained $\mu_{\mathrm{firm}}$, a $V$ its members adopt within a zone of acceptance, and a party who owns the difference between value realised and value paid out.

Inside a shared reference the agreement machinery operates (tilts are mutually legible and coordination is cheap) so firms are agreement zones; across references only the price survives as a common signal. This is Hayek's thesis [Hayek, 1945] given its formal statement: the market is coordination at minimal task-specific shared doxa, price carrying the margin against a shared institutional background that is itself a $\mu^{(3)}$ concentration, and the firm is coordination by deep shared doxa. The two are poles of one trade-off, between maintaining a reference and paying per-transaction tilts across the gap between references.

Inward, the exclusion law applies unchanged: $\mathrm{supp}(\rho^*)=\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{\mathrm{firm}})$, so configurations the corporate reference excludes are unreachable at any internal incentive — the innovator's dilemma as a one-line identity. In the AI transition a fleet of instances of one model shares its reference by weight-copying, so both poles of the trade-off collapse at once; the free shared reference is a monoculture, and the resilience law prices the winning design.

From section 5: the Coase theorem [Coase, 1960] reads against the physical component of the kinetic cost: when reallocation is cheap relative to the free-energy differences at stake, the relaxation reaches $\rho^*$ from any initial allocation and the starting point does not matter for efficiency; when it is dear, the relaxation is slow and path-dependent, and initial allocations matter because $\rho^*$ may not be reached in finite time. That is the identification. No quantitative Kramers formula is on the page.
