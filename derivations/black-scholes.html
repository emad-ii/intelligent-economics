Black–Scholes identified

In the complete Black–Scholes setting the risk-neutral measure is the Girsanov exponential tilt of the physical measure of asset returns, the same structural move fixed by no-arbitrage rather than by preference, at a relative-entropy cost of the same form as the complexity cost $C$. Completeness makes that measure unique. Incompleteness prices the reference.

The paper identifies the complete market's minimal-entropy measure with the forced exponential tilt, the constraint now no-arbitrage (Table 3; section 5; film IV). No option formula is written in the paper, and none is added here.

$$\rho^*\propto\mu\,e^{\lambda R}\qquad\text{s.t. no-arbitrage},\qquad C=\tau\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\rho^*\Vert\mu)$$

What Table 3 returns

Leave the market complete and drag the physical weights: $\rho^*$ stays put, only the relative-entropy cost moves. Switch the market incomplete: the same slider now moves $\rho^*$ itself. That is incompleteness pricing the reference. $\tau$ prices the KL, the same form as $C$; it does not write an option.

What the paper states

From section 5: in the complete Black–Scholes setting [Black and Scholes, 1973], the risk-neutral measure is the Girsanov exponential tilt of the physical measure of asset returns, the same structural move fixed by no-arbitrage rather than by preference, at a relative-entropy cost of the same form as the complexity cost $C$. Table 3 lists the recovery as the complete market's minimal-entropy measure, and the interest it returns as incompleteness prices the reference.

From the boxed result of section 5.1: the physical measure tilted to no-arbitrage gives Black–Scholes. The unifying fact is structural: bounded-rational choice, the risk-neutral measure, and the maximum-entropy logit are one object, the unique exponential tilt of a reference fixed by a constraint, and the classical equations are what it yields under the references each field assumes. Film IV on ie.ii.inc is that no-arbitrage tilt. The site recovery map names the same row: complete markets assumed; the tilt's relative-entropy cost has the same form as $C$.

The paper does not write a call price, a PDE, or a $d_1,d_2$ formula. Those setting-specific constructions are not added here. Path-space Girsanov already priced $K_{\mathrm{info}}$ in section 3; this page is the asset-return identification, not a second kinetic term.
