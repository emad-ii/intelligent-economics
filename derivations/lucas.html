Lucas / Dual Engine forced

A policy change is a change in $V$. Within a round the realised distribution relaxes toward $\rho^*\propto\mu\,e^{V/\tau}$; across rounds the settled distribution becomes the next reference: $\mu\leftarrow\rho^*$. That Dual Engine is the equation of motion the Lucas critique said was missing. The adaptation rate is a parameter.

The across-round map $\mu\leftarrow\rho^*$ is forced (section 5.1; Fig. 6). Slow adaptation recovers fixed expectations; fast adaptation recovers rational expectations; real economies sit between. This page is that motion. The same map with a support-restriction step is Akerlof; that page is separate, and is not re-run here.

The two limits the critique faced

Park the adaptation rate at zero: $\mu$ does not move, fixed expectations. Drive it to one and add rounds: the reference anneals toward a point mass on the policy peak, long-run rational expectations, and the doxic concentration section 8 prices. Move the policy peak; $\mu$ is not invariant. What matters for policy is this rate, which is estimable.

What the paper states

From section 5.1: Lucas observed that the parameters of an estimated policy relationship are not invariant to policy: agents' expectations adapt when the regime changes, so an exploited correlation shifts. Rational-expectations macroeconomics answered by assuming expectations are already model-consistent, immune to the critique by construction and empirically too fast. Expectations live in $\mu$, and the dynamics says how the realised distribution adjusts when $V$ changes.

Expectations relax to the new equilibrium. A policy change is a change in $V$. Within a round, the realised distribution relaxes toward the new target $\rho^*\propto\mu\,e^{V/\tau}$ by the overdamped Langevin diffusion of Link 4, $dX_t=\nabla\log\rho^*(X_t)\,dt+\sqrt{2}\,dW_t$, at a finite rate set by $\tau$ and the geometry of the score; across rounds, the settled distribution becomes the updated reference, so $\mu$ itself moves by absorbing what the relaxation reaches. The Langevin step within a round holds $\mu$ fixed; it is this across-round update that carries the change in expectations. Iterated to settlement under a fixed policy, the update anneals the reference toward a point mass on the optimum: long-run rational expectations as the limiting case, and, at the limit itself, the doxic concentration whose fragility section 8 prices. The Lucas critique is the statement that $\mu$ is not policy-invariant, and the two steps together provide an equation of motion for the adjustment of expectations the critique says is missing.

The dichotomy the standard treatment faces, fixed expectations or instantly rational ones, is the two limits of this one relaxation: slow adaptation recovers fixed expectations, fast adaptation recovers rational expectations, real economies sit between. What matters for policy is the adaptation rate, which is estimable. So the framework turns the Lucas critique from a prohibition on policy analysis into a parameter to be measured. The reference is what the system brings before the current value computation; once a round settles, what it brings to the next round is the distribution the last round left, $\mu\leftarrow\rho^*$. Fig. 6 on ie.ii.inc is that iteration. Table 3 lists rational expectations as the fixed point of $\mu\leftarrow\rho^*$, and the interest it returns as the Lucas critique as motion.

The same across-round iteration, run by a system on its own output, is the noiseless skeleton of model collapse [Shumailov et al., 2024]; with a support-restriction step it is Akerlof. Those two readings share the map and have their own page. This page is the Dual Engine and the missing equation of motion.
