What the paper states

From section 6.1: couple the agents through a shared resource constraint $\sum_i\mathbb{E}_{\rho_i}[x_i]=\omega$. The multiplier enters every stationarity condition identically, and each optimal law is $\rho_i^*(x)\propto\mu_i(x)\exp\bigl((V_i(x)-p\cdot x)/\tau_i\bigr)$, the price tilt of the demand recovery, now derived. The price is to the shared constraint what the temperature is to the information constraint: both are Lagrange multipliers.

Clearing is then a convex program. Aggregate excess demand is the negative gradient of $\Phi(p)=\sum_a\tau_a\log Z_a(p)+p\cdot\omega$, the sum running over both sides of the market, producers entering through $e^{(p\cdot y-c(y))/\tau}$. $\Phi$ is convex because every log-partition function is. On compactly supported references with the endowment interior to the aggregate achievable set, $\Phi$ is finite, smooth, and coercive, so equilibrium prices exist as its minimiser, are unique whenever the covariance Hessian is non-degenerate, and tâtonnement (prices adjusting along excess demand) is a gradient flow that converges globally to them.

The classical pathologies then acquire an address rather than a mystery. Cooling $\tau\to 0$ collapses the curvature of $\Phi$ into kinks, which is where non-uniqueness and tâtonnement instability live; restoring income effects (wealth-dependent references or values) breaks the gradient structure itself, which is where the Sonnenschein-Mantel-Debreu indeterminacy lives. The scope is the quasi-linear economy, the same scope the Slutsky recovery declared. Table 3 lists the recovery as forced, and the interest it returns as dissipative tâtonnement, welfare at temperature. Fig. 7 on ie.ii.inc is this picture: a ball rolling down a convex bowl.
