Landauer's bound identified

Erasing a bit takes a two-state distribution to a single state. Against a uniform reference that raises $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\rho\Vert\mu)$ by exactly $\ln 2$, and $C=\tau\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ charges $\tau$ per nat, so the cost is $\tau\ln 2$. Physically instantiated against the Gibbs state, $\tau$ is $k_B T$ and $\tau\ln 2=k_B T\ln 2$: Landauer's bound. The identification is an inheritance.

The form $\tau\ln 2$ is forced by the complexity cost already in the free energy. The magnitude is the physical identification $\tau=k_B T$ (section 7.1; Part V; Table 3). That is the physical floor of $C$.

What the paper states

From section 7.1: erasing a bit takes a distribution supported on two equiprobable states to one supported on a single state. Against a uniform two-state reference this erasure raises $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\rho\Vert\mu)$ by exactly $\ln 2$ nats, and the complexity cost $C=\tau D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ of the free energy charges $\tau$ per nat, so the cost of the erasure is $\tau\ln 2$. When the computation is physically instantiated at temperature $T$ with the reference taken as the physical Gibbs state, the information temperature $\tau$ is the thermodynamic temperature $k_B T$, and the cost is $\tau\ln 2=k_B T\ln 2$, which is Landauer's bound.

The identification is an inheritance: the nonequilibrium free energy of a physically instantiated distribution exceeds its equilibrium value by exactly $k_B T$ times the KL divergence from the Gibbs state [Parrondo et al., 2015], which is the complexity cost $C$ evaluated against the physical reference. Under the identification of the information temperature $\tau$ with the thermodynamic temperature $k_B T$, which holds when computation is physically instantiated against the Gibbs state, the KL term of the free energy evaluated on a one-bit support reduction returns Landauer's cost directly. The thermodynamic floor on $c_{\mathrm{sub}}$ is therefore located in form by the framework and in magnitude by the physical identification: the machine's instantiation cost falls toward $k_B T\ln 2$ per irreversible operation, the human's sits far above it and is metabolically pinned, and both floors are the same complexity cost the chain already forced.

Table 3 lists the recovery as two states, $\tau=k_B T$, and the interest it returns as the physical floor of $C$. Part V on ie.ii.inc is that floor. The form $\tau\ln 2$ is forced; the magnitude comes from identifying the information temperature with the physical one.
