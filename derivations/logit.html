Multinomial logit forced

The forced object on a finite uniform support is McFadden’s logit. $\tau$ is the information price, not a fitting constant. Restoring a non-flat $\mu$ returns the doxic term in the log-odds.

The paper recovers the logit as this object on a finite uniform support, with $\tau$ an information price rather than a fitting constant (Table 3; section 5; film II). The $V$ numbers here are illustrative, as on ie.ii.inc; only the mechanism is the paper’s.

$$\rho_i=\frac{\mu_i\,e^{V_i/\tau}}{\sum_j\mu_j\,e^{V_j/\tau}},\qquad \log\frac{\rho_i}{\rho_j}=\frac{V_i-V_j}{\tau}+\log\frac{\mu_i}{\mu_j}$$

Uniform $\mu$ kills the second term and is the classical logit. A restored $\mu$ puts the doxic term back in the log-odds. That is what Table 3 says $\tau>0$ returns.

$\tau$ as information price

Choice $\rho$

Reference $\mu$

Five alternatives, finite support. $V=(3.0,2.4,3.6,1.2,2.1)$. Restored $\mu=(0.35,0.30,0.05,0.20,0.10)$ matches the illustrative numbers on the official site. C is the value-max option.

Price, not a fit

$E_\rho[V]$
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\rho\|\mu)$
$C=\tau\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}$

$\tau$ prices departure from the reference in units of value. Drag it: low $\tau$ concentrates on $C$; high $\tau$ pays less per nat and stays nearer $\mu$. That is the information price, not a scale chosen to fit shares.

Log-odds, C against A

With uniform $\mu$ the doxic term is zero and the log-odds are $(V_i-V_j)/\tau$. Uncheck flatten and the second term appears. Table 3’s returned interest: the doxic term in the log-odds.

What the paper states

From section 5: the remaining recoveries are carried at the level the deductive line requires and no further. The logit is this object on a finite uniform support, with $\tau$ an information price rather than a fitting constant. Table 3 lists the recovery as forced, recovered by a uniform $\mu$ on finite alternatives, and the interest it returns as the doxic term in the log-odds. Film II on ie.ii.inc is this derivation.
