Welfare at temperature forced

By convex duality the clearing allocation maximises aggregate free energy. That is a first welfare theorem at temperature. The second follows by transfers in the numéraire. Both are doxa-conditional: Pareto is blind to $\mu$.

The paper reads welfare as value net of information costs (Table 3; section 6.1; Part IV on ie.ii.inc). The classical surplus theorem is the $\tau\to 0$ shadow. Efficiency inherits the reference: an allocation is efficient against a given doxa.

What the paper states

From section 6.1: by convex duality the clearing allocation maximises aggregate free energy $\sum_a\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\rho_a}[V_a]-\tau_a D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\rho_a\|\mu_a)\bigr)$ over all feasible allocations: a first welfare theorem at temperature, welfare read as value net of information costs, with the classical surplus theorem as its $\tau\to 0$ shadow and the second theorem following by transfers in the numéraire.

What the derivation exposes is what Pareto cannot see. The maximised objective is conditional on the reference profile: an allocation is efficient against a given doxa, a group whose reference excludes some goods receives no weight there at any price, and the outcome still certifies as efficient. Efficiency inherits the reference. The welfare theorems are therefore doxa-conditional, and the normative content they cannot carry (who is in the support) lives in the viability laws of section 4, which are prior to Pareto because Pareto is blind to $\mu$. Table 3 lists clearing and welfare together as forced, and the interest they return as dissipative tâtonnement, welfare at temperature. Part IV on ie.ii.inc is this recovery.
